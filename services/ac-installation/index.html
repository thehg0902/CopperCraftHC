Air-Conditioning Installation

We install and replace central air systems matched to your home — the condenser outside, the coil and blower inside, all working as one system.

Why sizing drives comfort

  • In our humid summers, sizing matters more than raw capacity.
  • An oversized unit cools the air fast but shuts off before it removes the moisture.
  • The result is a house that is cold and damp at the same time.

Helpful to know

  • Size for humidity removal, not just square footage — bigger is not better with air conditioning.
  • The indoor coil and blower need to match the new outdoor unit; pairing new equipment with mismatched old parts costs efficiency.
  • Ask what the efficiency rating actually means for your home, in plain terms.
  • Placement matters: shade helps, clearance is essential, and keeping the unit away from bedroom windows keeps nights quiet.

Common Questions

Can central air be added to a house that never had it?

Often yes, where there is existing ductwork to work with. Where there is not, a ductless system is a genuine alternative rather than a consolation prize. Which one suits depends on the house.

Where does the outdoor unit have to go?

Somewhere with clearance on every side for airflow and service access, on a level pad, and ideally not directly under a bedroom window. On narrow lots this is worth settling before equipment is ordered.

Can I keep my existing furnace and just add cooling?

Usually yes. Central air shares the furnace blower and ductwork, so the indoor coil goes on top of the existing furnace. It is worth checking the furnace's age at the same time, so you are not paying twice for the same labour a year apart.
