Furnace Repair

Won't start, short-cycles, or blows cold air? We find the actual cause — ignition, sensors, blower, airflow — and explain it in plain language before any work goes ahead.

Why it matters

  • Most winter breakdowns start small: a dirty flame sensor, a clogged filter, a worn part working harder than it should.
  • Caught early, those are quick fixes.
  • Ignored, they become no-heat calls on the coldest night in January.

Helpful to know

  • Check the filter monthly during heating season — a clogged filter is one of the most common causes of weak heat and short-cycling.
  • Before assuming the worst, check the thermostat batteries, the furnace power switch and the breaker.
  • Note what the furnace is doing — short-cycling, cold air, a new noise — and when it started. That pattern speeds up the diagnosis.
  • If you smell gas or a carbon-monoxide alarm sounds, leave the house first and call your gas utility — before calling anyone else.

Common Questions

How quickly can you get here for a no-heat call?

During business hours we prioritise no-heat situations and get to them as fast as we honestly can. CopperCraft does not run a 24/7 service, so an overnight message is answered the next working day rather than at 3 a.m.

Will you tell me if the furnace is not worth repairing?

Yes. If the equipment is old enough that parts are scarce, or the repair costs a meaningful fraction of a replacement, we will say so and lay out both options. The decision stays yours.

Is a strange smell when the heat first comes on normal?

Dust burning off the heat exchanger at the start of the season is common and passes within an hour or so. A persistent burning, electrical or sulphur smell is not, and a gas smell means leaving the house and calling your gas utility first.
