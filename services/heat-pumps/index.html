Heat Pumps

A heat pump heats and cools with one system, moving heat instead of burning fuel. We size and install cold-climate models, often paired with a furnace in a hybrid setup.

What has actually changed

  • Modern cold-climate units heat efficiently well below freezing — a different machine from the heat pumps of twenty years ago.
  • For many homes here the practical answer is hybrid.
  • The heat pump handles most days; the furnace backs it up in a deep freeze.

Helpful to know

  • Look for cold-climate ratings — they matter for Richmond Hill winters.
  • A hybrid setup switches automatically to whichever heat source makes sense at the moment.
  • If your impression of heat pumps was formed twenty years ago, they've earned a fresh look.
  • Rebate programs for heat-pump upgrades come and go — ask what's currently available and we'll tell you what actually applies.

Also serving heat pumps in Aurora, Vaughan and Markham.

Common Questions

Do heat pumps actually work through an Ontario winter?

Modern cold-climate units keep useful output well below freezing, and most Ontario installs pair them with a backup heat source for the coldest stretches. The design matters more than the technology.

Is a heat pump worth it for my house?

Sometimes clearly yes, sometimes clearly no. It depends on your existing ductwork, the state of the current system and how the house holds heat. We will show you the comparison rather than push one answer.

Do I still need a furnace with a heat pump?

Most Ontario installs keep a backup heat source for the coldest stretches, which is often the existing furnace. That pairing is called a hybrid or dual-fuel system, and it is frequently the most practical answer here.
