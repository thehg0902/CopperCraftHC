Heating

Furnace Repair

When a furnace won't start, short-cycles or blows cold air, we diagnose the actual cause — ignition, sensors, blower, airflow — and explain what we found in plain language before any work goes ahead.

Most winter breakdowns start as small faults: a dirty flame sensor, a clogged filter, a worn part working harder than it should. Caught early, they're quick fixes. Ignored, they tend to become no-heat calls on the coldest night in January.

Helpful to know

  • Check the filter monthly during heating season — a clogged filter is one of the most common causes of weak heat and short-cycling.
  • Before assuming the worst, check the thermostat batteries, the furnace power switch and the breaker.
  • Note what the furnace is doing — short-cycling, cold air, a new noise — and when it started. That pattern speeds up the diagnosis.
  • If you smell gas or a carbon-monoxide alarm sounds, leave the house first and call your gas utility — before calling anyone else.

Furnace Installation

If your furnace is nearing the end of its run, we help you choose a replacement sized to your home — not just matched to the old unit's label — and install it cleanly, with the ductwork and venting done right.

Most furnaces reach end-of-life around 15–20 years. Getting the size right matters more than the brand on the box: an oversized furnace short-cycles, heats unevenly and wears itself out faster.

Helpful to know

  • Ask for a heat-loss calculation, not "the same size as the old one" — homes change, and older furnaces were often oversized to begin with.
  • When repair bills start stacking up on an older unit, ask for an honest repair-versus-replace comparison.
  • Two-stage and variable-speed models run longer, quieter and more evenly — worth understanding before you decide.
  • If you can, replace in spring or fall. A planned shoulder-season swap beats an emergency one in a cold snap.

Cooling

Air-Conditioning Repair

Not cooling, leaking, icing up or barely blowing? We test the system, find the fault — refrigerant, capacitor, coil, airflow — and fix what's actually wrong.

An air conditioner struggling through a humid Ontario July is under real strain. Run hard with a small fault, a cheap fix can turn into compressor damage — the kind of repair nobody wants to need.

Helpful to know

  • Keep the outdoor coil clear of grass clippings, leaves and cottonwood fluff — a gentle rinse with the power off helps it breathe.
  • If you see ice on the refrigerant line or the unit, shut the system off and let it thaw before the visit — running it iced makes things worse.
  • "It cools, but the house feels clammy" is a real symptom worth mentioning, not a complaint to sit on.
  • Don't box the condenser in with fencing or planters — it needs open airflow on all sides to do its job.

Cooling

Air-Conditioning Installation

We install and replace central air systems matched to your home — the condenser outside, the coil and blower inside, all working as one system.

In our humid summers, sizing drives comfort. An oversized unit cools the air fast but shuts off before it removes the moisture, leaving the house cold and damp at the same time.

Helpful to know

  • Size for humidity removal, not just square footage — bigger is not better with air conditioning.
  • The indoor coil and blower need to match the new outdoor unit; pairing new equipment with mismatched old parts costs efficiency.
  • Ask what the efficiency rating actually means for your home, in plain terms.
  • Placement matters: shade helps, clearance is essential, and keeping the unit away from bedroom windows keeps nights quiet.

Heat pumps

Heat Pumps

A heat pump heats and cools with one system, moving heat instead of burning fuel. We size and install cold-climate models, often paired with a furnace in a hybrid setup.

Modern cold-climate heat pumps heat efficiently well below freezing — a different machine from the heat pumps of twenty years ago. For many homes here, the practical answer is hybrid: the heat pump handles most days, and the furnace backs it up in a deep freeze.

Helpful to know

  • Look for cold-climate ratings — they matter for Richmond Hill winters.
  • A hybrid setup switches automatically to whichever heat source makes sense at the moment.
  • If your impression of heat pumps was formed twenty years ago, they've earned a fresh look.
  • Rebate programs for heat-pump upgrades come and go — ask what's currently available and we'll tell you what actually applies.

Ductless comfort

Ductless Systems

Ductless mini-splits bring heating and cooling to rooms your ductwork never reached — additions, finished basements, garages turned offices, and homes with boiler or radiator heat.

Instead of one thermostat fighting over the whole house, each indoor head controls its own space. It's also one of the cleanest air-conditioning answers for radiator-heated homes with no ducts at all.

Helpful to know

  • One outdoor unit can serve several indoor heads — one system, several zones.
  • The filters are washable: a seasonal rinse keeps airflow and efficiency up.
  • Where the indoor head goes affects comfort more than most people expect — placement is worth a conversation.

Water heating

Water Heaters

We repair and replace water heaters — conventional tanks and tankless — including the venting and plumbing connections around them.

Water heaters rarely fail politely. Rumbling, rusty hot water or moisture around the base are early warnings — and acting on them is a lot better than a burst tank and a flooded floor.

Helpful to know

  • Rumbling or popping usually means sediment build-up making the tank work harder than it should.
  • Rusty hot water or dampness at the base? Have it looked at before it becomes a leak.
  • Know where the water heater's shut-off valve is before you ever need it.
  • Tank versus tankless is a real trade-off — endless hot water and floor space on one side, up-front complexity and hard-water maintenance on the other.

Controls

Thermostats and Controls

We install and troubleshoot thermostats and controls — from simple programmable units to smart thermostats — wired correctly and set up for the system you actually have.

The thermostat is the brain of your heating and cooling. Poor placement or the wrong setup causes uneven temperatures, wasted run-time and comfort complaints no amount of equipment can fix.

Helpful to know

  • Many smart thermostats need a C-wire for power — worth checking before you buy one.
  • A programmed schedule beats big manual setbacks you have to remember.
  • If you have a heat pump, keep setbacks mild — deep ones can trigger expensive backup heat.
  • Keep the thermostat away from direct sun, drafts and supply vents; it can only react to the air right around it.

Maintenance

Preventative Maintenance

A seasonal maintenance visit inspects, cleans and tests your system — furnace in the fall, air conditioning in the spring — so worn parts are caught before they fail.

Equipment rarely fails at a convenient time; it fails at the season's peak, when it's working hardest. Regular service keeps small issues small — and many manufacturers expect annual service records to keep the warranty intact.

Helpful to know

  • Book the furnace check in fall and the A/C check in spring, ahead of the seasonal rush.
  • Change or check filters between visits — the single most useful thing a homeowner can do.
  • Keep about 60 cm of clearance around outdoor equipment, all year round.
  • Mention new noises or smells early, and note when they started — that history is genuinely useful.

Commercial

Light Commercial HVAC

We service and install heating and cooling for offices, shops, restaurants and other small commercial spaces — including rooftop units and back-room equipment.

Comfort affects staff, customers and stock, and commercial units run far more hours than a home system. The rooftop unit nobody looks at tends to get noticed only on the day it quits.

Helpful to know

  • Longer run-hours mean filters and belts need a tighter schedule than at home.
  • Seasonal service can be planned around your business hours, not through them.
  • Knowing the unit's make, model and age speeds up any service call — a photo of the data plate is enough.
  • Hot or cold complaints from one area are an early warning, not a nuisance — they usually mean something specific.
