Two Aurora Housing Stocks, Two Conversations

North along Yonge from our Richmond Hill shop. Aurora is two housing stocks that need two different conversations.

  • Near Yonge, Wellington and Town Park: access is the constraint

    • Many houses predate central air, and some predate ducted heating.
    • Where systems were added later they went wherever space existed.
    • Any replacement is limited by physical access as much as by capacity.

  • Century homes: the envelope often masquerades as the equipment

    • Early glazing, thin attic insulation and rim-joist leakage all make a correct furnace look undersized.
    • Replacing the furnace does not change any of that.
    • If that is what we find, we will say so before you spend the money.

  • East of Bayview: first replacements coming due

    • Aurora Heights and Bayview Wellington are consistent construction from the late 1990s onward.
    • Ducted from day one, so these are usually straightforward decisions.
    • A good moment to price a heat pump against a like-for-like swap.

  • Age alone is not a reason to replace

    • A twenty-year-old furnace that still runs well may not need replacing yet.
    • Aurora is far enough north that shoulder-season performance shows up, so get it assessed rather than guess.

Aurora Questions We Get Asked

My century home is drafty. Will a new furnace fix it?

Probably not on its own. Air leakage and insulation are separate problems from equipment, and replacing a furnace will not change them. We will tell you if that is what we find.

Can central air be added to a house that never had it?

Often yes, and where ductwork does not exist or cannot be run, ductless is a genuine alternative. Which one makes sense depends on the house, not on a standard package.

My subdivision furnace is about twenty years old and still runs. Replace it now?

Not automatically. Age alone is not a reason. It is a good moment to get it assessed and to price a heat pump against a like-for-like swap, so the decision is planned instead of forced.

Sometimes the Answer Is Not New Equipment

If insulation or air sealing is what is actually making your house uncomfortable, a new furnace will not solve it. We would rather lose the sale and tell you that than install equipment you did not need.
