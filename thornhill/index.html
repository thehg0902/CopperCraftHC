Why Thornhill Is Its Own Category

Thornhill sits on Yonge just south of us, and most of it was built decades before the subdivisions further north. That changes almost everything.

  • Mid-century houses: the ductwork is the limit, not the furnace

    • Bungalows, sidesplits and backsplits from the 1950s to 1970s, often renovated more than once.
    • The mechanicals have been replaced at least twice. The ductwork almost never has.
    • Builders ran generous supply and a single central return, which a modern high-efficiency furnace cannot work with.
    • Static pressure climbs, and the homeowner gets told to buy a bigger unit.

  • Split levels defeat a single thermostat

    • Half-storey offsets mean no one location represents the whole house.
    • It is why so many Thornhill homeowners describe one level as permanently wrong.
    • Usually improvable through controls and airflow balance rather than new equipment.

  • Radiators and no ducts? Cooling is still possible

    • Some older Thornhill homes are on hot water heating and have never had ductwork at all.
    • Ductless is the normal answer, and it can be zoned room by room.
    • It is a different quote from central air and should not be sold as one.

  • What we will not do

    • Recommend more capacity before anyone has checked static pressure.
    • Sell you a larger system that will run into exactly the same wall.

Thornhill Questions We Get Asked

My house has radiators and no ducts. Can I still get air conditioning?

Yes. Ductless mini-split systems are the usual answer for hot-water-heated Thornhill homes, and they can be zoned room by room. It is a different quote from a conventional central air install.

I was told I need a bigger furnace. Is that right?

In mid-century Thornhill houses it frequently is not. Undersized return air makes a correctly sized furnace behave like a small one. We check static pressure before recommending more capacity.

One level of my split-level is always wrong. Is that fixable?

Usually improvable, sometimes fully fixable. Split levels defeat a single thermostat location, so the fix is normally about controls and airflow balance rather than new equipment.

A Bigger Furnace Is Rarely the Fix

In houses of this vintage the equipment is frequently not the problem. We would rather tell you the ductwork is the limiting factor than sell you a larger system that will run into exactly the same wall.
