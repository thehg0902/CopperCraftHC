Big Houses Are a Different Engineering Problem

Woodbridge, Maple, Kleinburg and the VMC. A bigger house is a different engineering problem, not just a bigger unit.

  • Oversizing is the expensive mistake here

    • Kleinburg and newer Woodbridge homes run well above the York Region average in volume.
    • An oversized system short cycles and never runs long enough to dehumidify.
    • It also wears out faster than the correctly sized alternative.
    • Capacity chosen by square footage alone is money you pay to run for the next fifteen years.

  • One floor comfortable and another not? That is distribution, not capacity

    • Upstairs bakes in July while the basement stays cold.
    • The fix is usually zoning, a two-stage or variable-speed system, or returns the original build never included.
    • A larger furnace makes it worse, not better.

  • Older Woodbridge near Highway 7: the addition nobody can heat

    • Smaller postwar and 1970s houses, many of them extended.
    • Additions were frequently never tied properly into the original duct layout.

  • VMC and stacked towns: rules and space, not square footage

    • What fits the mechanical closet decides the equipment.
    • What the building permits on a balcony or roof decides the rest.

Vaughan Questions We Get Asked

Upstairs is hot and the basement is cold. Do I need a bigger AC?

Almost never. That pattern usually points at air distribution rather than capacity, and a larger unit tends to make it worse by short cycling. Zoning, added returns or a variable-speed system are the more common fixes.

Is a heat pump worth it on a large Vaughan home?

Often, but it depends on the house and how the existing ductwork performs. It is worth pricing against a conventional replacement rather than assuming either answer.

Can you work on a unit in a Vaughan Metropolitan Centre condo?

It depends on the equipment type and what the building permits. Tell us the unit type when you call and we will let you know before booking whether it is work we can take on.
